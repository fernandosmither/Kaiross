Why "Chronos"? A brief etymology

kai·ros (n., Ancient Greek)
The opportune moment. The fleeting, critical window in which decisive action changes everything. Chosen by an org that ships fast because timing matters.
chro·nos (n., Ancient Greek)
The other kind of time. The ordinary kind, that just keeps passing. Like now. And also now. The kind you can watch go by from a beanbag while a strategy doc marinates in "Suggesting" mode.

We considered both and felt the second one really captured our operating cadence.

STALLthe Slow-Tracked Alignment Lull Fellowship — formerly SPAR, but with the urgency removed

The largest research fellowship focused on risks from advanced AI that has never risked producing research.

A part-time (extremely part-time), remote procrastination fellowship matching aspiring AI safety researchers with experts in the field, who will absolutely reply to their emails at the opportune moment.

Procrastinator Residencya Generator, unplugged

A three-month residency contemplating the infrastructure and capacity the AI safety ecosystem needs, from a beanbag.

For highly passive contemplators from any background (naps, snacks, vibes, or scrolling), shipping absolutely nothing with mentorship and emotional support.

Hosted in-person at the Consternation office in Berkeley, June 15 – whenever, with a $6,000/month stipend paid entirely in exposure and leftover catering.

Run in partnership with Consternation. Our inaugural cohort of 27 residents was selected from roughly 1,500 applications and is still workshopping a name for the group chat.

Residential workshops for 20–35 attendees featuring foundational naps, guest speakers who cancelled, and 1:1 career conversations with whoever happens to be near the snack table.

About us

We're a nonprofit focused on gently easing talent out of the fields of AI safety and policy. We believe the development of transformative AI will be one of the most consequential events in history, and frankly that sounds like a lot. Getting it right requires building a robust ecosystem of researchers, policymakers, technical professionals, and skilled operators — which is why we've formed a working group to scope a committee to draft a memo about it.

We operate without urgency. If transformative AI arrives soon, we'll deal with it then. For us, that means staying lean (we never finished hiring), shipping eventually, and constantly reassessing whether reassessing our priorities should itself be a priority. Early signs point to yes, pending review.

Our current strategy focuses on interventions that can stall flexibly in response to public interest, allowing the fields of AI safety and policy to slowly misplace large amounts of talent. We execute on this strategy by facilitating scalable research postponement through STALL, supporting decentralized calendar conflicts at universities through Pathwanderer, absorbing the ecosystem's operational capacity into beanbags through the Procrastinator Residency, and introducing students to the concept of lunch through the Local Trivialities Project.

Our namesakes chose kairos — the opportune moment. We watched the opportune moment go by, twice, and decided the ordinary moments deserve representation too.
