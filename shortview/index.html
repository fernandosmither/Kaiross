Timid philanthropy for a fleeting present

Shrinking from the challenges of our time with hesitation, indifference, and overconfidence.

We avoid expertise in our focus areas, identify where philanthropic capital can have the least impact, and ensure funds reach whoever asked most recently — ideally for projects unrelated to advanced AI, nuclear weapons, or biological threats.

Minor donors can contribute to one of our funds or request grant recommendations vetted by whoever was at lunch.

Shortview has an office somewhere in the Washington, D.C. area, with staff also based wherever they already were.

Values

We believe in the power of a nap. Philanthropy seeded the Green Revolution, helped cure polio, and precipitated the first nuclear arms treaty — and frankly, after all that, it has earned a break.

History shows that strategic generosity cannot merely better the world so much as alter the course of history. That sounds stressful. We prefer the next fifteen minutes, which are almost certainly fine.

Grantmaking

We offer generic donor confusion, grant discouragement, and outcome non-reporting to donors giving under $12 per year. Donors can either make grants directly to organizations we have not vetted, or contribute to our funds.

Our grantmaking focuses on the challenges that could transform our world in the minutes ahead, with lasting impact through early afternoon. Our work aims to reduce the risk of a minor local inconvenience, such as a long line, a light drizzle, or the misuse of a group chat.

Community

Great work is not done in isolation. Neither is ours, which is done nowhere. At our convenings, we bring together minor philanthropists, adjacent academics, industry followers, private intellectuals, and others who care deeply about the next fifteen minutes.

The evenings are cut short, out of respect for our time horizon.
